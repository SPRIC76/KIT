The short version

KiT vs Night Shift

Both keep the PC awake while the room goes dark so overnight AI or other long work can continue. That is the overlap. Night Shift is a tray app built around that bedtime combo: Turn Off Monitors, Keep System Awake, and Monitors Off + Stay Awake. It powers the monitors off, holds the machine awake with a Windows power request, wakes when you move the mouse, then shows a stats popup for how long the monitors were off. It also ships macOS and Linux scripts alongside a Windows installer.

KiT is a Windows 10/11 product with three named modes and a global chord each: Light (Ctrl+Shift+D — PC and screen stay awake), Night (Ctrl+Shift+K — dark + awake), Off (hold either — ordinary Windows). A slim bar above the taskbar shows the mode; Ghost makes that bar transparent and click-through; triple-tap flips Light↔Night. Night can show moving faces (Cosmos, Orbit, Snake, DVD, or Random) so nothing burns in, and it covers every monitor. Auto after idle (1–30 minutes), Hold Night until Esc or Space, and Night / monitor brightness live in the tray. KiT never fakes input, needs no admin or account, sends no telemetry, and publishes SHA-256 beside each download.

KiT is the better fit when you want Light and Night and Off as one instrument on Windows, with a mode bar and Night faces. Night Shift is the better fit when you want a tiny tray combo that physically turns monitors off, a monitor-off stats popup, and the same idea on macOS or Linux via scripts.

Where KiT is stronger (checkable)

Only features you can open and confirm — not speed or ratings.

  • Three named modes, one chord each. Light keeps the screen on; Night darkens it while the PC works; Off returns ordinary Windows. Night Shift’s public menu is monitors off, stay awake, and the combo — not a dedicated keep-the-screen-on Light mode.
  • Always-visible mode bar. KiT sits above the taskbar, with optional Ghost (transparent, click-through) and triple-tap Light↔Night. Night Shift is tray- and menu-oriented: left-click turns monitors off; right-click opens the menu. No documented global chords.
  • Dark with motion, or quiet dark. Night Shift turns monitors off. KiT Night can power the desk into wakeable faces that keep moving so nothing burns in, covering every monitor — or stay dark without the theater. Those are different ways to keep the room dark; KiT is the better fit when you want on-screen Night faces rather than a powered-off panel.
  • First-class idle and hold. Auto after idle (1–30 minutes) and Hold Night until Esc or Space, plus Night and monitor brightness, are tray settings on KiT. Night Shift documents Run on Startup and the stats popup; it does not document auto-idle, Hold Night, or brightness controls.
  • Documented local posture on the product site. No telemetry, no network on its own (updates only when you click Check for updates), no admin, SHA-256 beside downloads, Scoop, portable KIT.exe or installer, and the public TRUST.md. Night Shift’s README does not make those packaging claims; its published Windows source has no network calls, and GitHub lists asset digests on the release API.

KiT vs Caffeine vs PowerToys Awake

Caffeine’s default trick is fake input: a simulated F15 key every 59 seconds so Windows thinks you are still typing. That is small and familiar, and it can surprise apps that see the key. PowerToys Awake is the grown-up keep-awake inside Microsoft PowerToys: it requests a real awake state, can leave the screen off on a timeout, and lives in a large suite with telemetry you can turn off. KiT is one ~22 MB program with Light, Night, and Off. It never fakes input, needs no admin or account, and sends no telemetry.

Pick PowerToys Awake if you already run PowerToys and only need “don’t sleep.” Pick KiT when the job is stay-awake plus a dark room, without a suite and without simulated keys.

Use KiT Night. One chord darkens every display while the PC keeps working — overnight downloads, renders, or agents included.
